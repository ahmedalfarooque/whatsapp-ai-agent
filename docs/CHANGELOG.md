## 2026-10-21 — Groq per-minute token limit: a too-large request is not a sick route
* **Evidence (production routing history, read-only):** Groq's free tier allows 8,000 tokens per minute for `openai/gpt-oss-20b`; the reply budget (1,500) counts against it. When prompt + budget exceeded the limit Groq answered HTTP 413 *"Request too large … Limit 8000, Requested …"*. That was classified as an unavailable model and the route was paused for 5 minutes, although a slightly smaller request would have been accepted — the only provider answering was taken out of service by its own request size.
* **Fix:** the full error message is read (`parseTokenLimit`); a "request too large" answer is category `context` and **never pauses the route**. When the prompt alone fits, the request is retried **once** with the reply budget that fits (limit − prompt − 100, never below 256); when the prompt alone is too large the request fails over to the next provider at once. A cumulative "Used … Requested …" limit stays a 429 rate limit and pauses as before. Groq's customer-reply budget is now 1,000 tokens (reasoning-only escalation: 2,000), leaving room under the 8,000 limit.
* **Not changed (decision recorded):** replay attempts still count every failure and a message left after three attempts goes to staff. Making provider-side failures free was tried and rejected: it breaks the "left for staff after three attempts" guarantee (`recoveryEscalation.e2e`) and could hold a message in `awaiting_ai` indefinitely.
* **Tests** (scripted providers — not real Groq behaviour): `reliabilityLifecycle.e2e` +5 (prompt too large → fail over, no pause; budget tips it over → one retry at 600 → Groq answers; used-up 429 still pauses; `parseTokenLimit`); Groq budget expectations updated to 1,000 / 2,000. Full suite 175 files / 1925 tests.
* Rollback: `git checkout -B master 0623cb4`, `npm run build`, restart `whatsapp-ai-agent` (no schema change).

## 2026-10-21 — reliability pass: delivery retry, reasoning-aware reply budgets, key validation, cross-process trial lease
* **Baseline verified first** (HEAD = origin = production = 31d4220; health/readiness/public 200; one Node process; 3005 not listening). Production routing history showed: OpenRouter data-policy 404s and 429s (operator-side: openrouter.ai privacy setting / free quota), Gemini answering again after the replay fix, **16 empty Groq answers with `finish_reason=length`** (customer replies at 600 tokens ran out on hidden reasoning), and 125 older assistant rows without delivery evidence.
* **Generated ≠ delivered.** When the AI answered and the WhatsApp send failed, the answer stayed stored as `send_failed` and nothing ever retried it while the service ran; after a restart the replay sweep even treated the stored answer as "answered meanwhile". New `src/replay/deliverySweep.ts` (runs before the replay sweep every 2 minutes): resends the SAME stored text through the account's live transport — no new AI call — at most 3 tries (counted before each try), only answers younger than 6 h, never after the customer received a newer delivered answer (marked `superseded`), a failing try sends nothing else, after the last try staff get one "reply could not be delivered" notification and the answer is `send_abandoned`; a late delivery closes the waiting question as answered.
* **Customer replies get a reasoning-aware budget.** `chatBudget` (`validationBudget.ts`, `chatTokens` in `vendors.ts`): Groq 1500, everyone else 600. A reply that comes back empty because the whole budget went on reasoning is retried ONCE on the same route with a doubled, bounded budget (never above 2048); still empty → failover; nothing blank or internal is ever sent. Explicit budgets (validation) are not touched.
* **Replacing a key is validated.** Settings edits with a new key send ONE real minimal request: a key the provider rejects is refused (HTTP 422, the old key stays); quota/outage/empty cannot prove the key wrong, so it is saved and the response says it could NOT be verified (never "verified"); a verified key records a passed test.
* **Cross-process safety.** The recovery trial is also a lease row in the database (`trial:<route>` in `ai_route_cooldowns`): a second worker (or a restarted process overlapping the old one during a deploy) cannot run its own trial against the same recovering route and fails over instead; leases expire on their own. Production runs a single Node process today; the lease makes an accidental second one safe. Backoff/failure counts were already database-backed.
* **`invalid_request` is its own failure class** (HTTP 400/422 that is not policy, capability or context): route-scoped backoff (5 → 60 min), its own label in Settings, never confused with quota, key or billing. "Unsupported value for this parameter" is no longer mistaken for a missing capability (that pattern rested a route for an hour).
* **Settings:** times the server states as UTC are shown in the operator's own time zone; the page shows persisted cooldowns, the active provider, the next retry and its basis (tested against a faked clock, including that nothing stale remains after recovery).
* **Tests** (scripted providers, mocked WhatsApp client, faked clocks — not real provider or WhatsApp behaviour): `reliabilityLifecycle.e2e` (10), `reliabilitySettings.e2e` (7). Full suite 174 files / 1911 tests.

## 2026-10-21 — provider limits: stated resets, bounded backoff, one trial at a time, one notice per outage (migration 033)
* **Quota is told apart from a bad key, billing and ordinary failures.** HTTP 429 / quota exhaustion is `rate_limited` (Gemini 429s used to be filed as billing); a rejected key is `auth` (provider paused 10 min, doubling to 1 h); HTTP 402 is `credit` (billing, 30 min doubling to 6 h); 5xx, timeouts and network errors are `provider_error`/`timeout`/`network` (1 min doubling to 15 min); a retired model rests an hour.
* **The provider's own reset is read and stored as given.** `Retry-After` (seconds or HTTP date), `retry-after-ms`, `X-RateLimit-Reset`, the "please try again in 7m12s" wording of OpenAI-protocol errors, Google's `RetryInfo` and the daily Pacific-midnight reset (up to 25 h; the old 15-minute cut-off is gone). The pause ends at that moment and is stored as `reset_known`; a short hint on a *daily* limit never makes the route retry within seconds.
* **No reset time → a bounded backoff, never an invented reset.** Doubles per consecutive failure up to a cap (1, 2, 4, 8, 15, 15 min for ordinary failures; 30 min → 2 h for daily quotas; 5 → 60 min for other quotas), starts over after a success or six quiet hours, and is displayed as an estimate. Sibling routes failing inside the same message count as one failure and never shorten a running pause.
* **Persisted across restarts** (`ai_route_cooldowns`, migration 033: category, `reset_known`, `strikes`, `set_at_ms`): the pause, whether its end was stated by the provider, and the failure count survive a restart; the row stays after the pause ends so the next failure continues the backoff.
* **A recovering provider is tested by ONE request at a time.** A route that failed before is, when its pause ends, tried by a single request; concurrent requests wait (up to 8 s) for that result — if it fails they fail over without touching the provider, if it succeeds they use it. Healthy routes are never serialised.
* **Order:** a new shared provider joins the end of Default (OpenRouter 10, Gemini 100, Groq 110 …); Groq is used only when configured **and** confirmed free (an unconfirmed provider is never called, even with every other route exhausted).
* **Customers:** the "assistant unavailable" notice is sent **once per outage and conversation**, tracked in the database (the old in-memory ten-minute window repeated it after 10 minutes and after every restart); a real AI answer ends the outage, a later one is announced again. Provider wording is never shown to a customer (tested with "AI limit finished" bodies); waiting questions are answered once by the recovery sweep when a provider is eligible again.
* **Super Admin Settings** shows the active provider (last answer), the provider the next message would use, and for a resting provider the next retry time with whether it is a reset the provider stated or an estimated backoff (per card and in the routing diagnostics).
* **Production finding fixed in the same release:** every question answered LATER by Gemini failed with HTTP 400 *"Requests ending with a model turn are not supported"* (routing history, 2026-10-21 15:06–15:09), because a replayed question is stored followed by the automated notice, so the conversation ended with an assistant turn; the 400 also rested the Gemini route. The agent loop now leaves trailing assistant turns out of what the model sees (the conversation ends with the customer's message) and the Gemini adapter never sends a request ending with a plain model turn. Also read from production: 24 h of Gemini timeouts are the 10-minute recovery probes during a Google 503 *"high demand"* period, not customer requests; five OpenRouter 401s at 13:42–13:44 UTC rested the provider for 10 minutes and it then answered again.
* **Tests** (scripted providers and a faked clock — not real provider behaviour): `providerLimitHandling.e2e` (19: classification and stored reset for 429/401/402/500 and Retry-After forms, doubling and cap, restart persistence, single trial under five concurrent requests, never-paid/unconfirmed Groq, all-exhausted single notice, webhook-duplicate guard, replay once after reset) plus updated unit tests. Full suite 171 files / 1884 tests.
* Rollback: `git checkout -B master e9ea871`, `npm run build`, restart `whatsapp-ai-agent` (migration 033's columns are additive and harmless to the previous build).
